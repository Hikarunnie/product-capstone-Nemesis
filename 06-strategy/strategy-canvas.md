## Narrative

The strategy canvas shows that existing alternatives force creators to trade simplicity for structure. Social platforms offer free reach and familiar posting, but selling remains dependent on algorithms, direct messages, and manual organisation. Store builders provide strong commerce infrastructure but require setup, branding, and traffic generation. Etsy supplies handmade discovery, yet it is global, fee-based, and not designed around a Georgian beginner’s first selling experience. Musa intentionally lowers investment in global reach, advanced customization, and complete transaction infrastructure during the MVP stage. It raises Georgian fit, handmade focus, guided onboarding, structured product publishing, and emotional safety for creators who hesitate to begin. Musa also creates a local “first studio” category: a public storefront that is simpler than building a shop and more organised than posting through social media. This curve is strategically useful only if Musa develops enough local inventory and trust. The next proof is repeat publishing, customer inquiries, and creator retention—not additional surface-level features.

## Strategic Test

The curve is validated only when Musa can show:

- creators publish their first listing faster and with less assistance;
- creators return to publish additional products;
- listings generate product views, studio visits, inquiries, or sales;
- local catalogue density improves buyer engagement;
- creators describe Musa as easier than their previous workaround.
