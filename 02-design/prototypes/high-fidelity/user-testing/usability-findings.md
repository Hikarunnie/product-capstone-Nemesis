# Usability Findings

## Product

**Musa** — a simple storefront and marketplace tool for handmade creators.

## Testing Goal

The goal of this usability test was to understand whether first-time users can understand Musa’s purpose, discover handmade products, and complete the main creator flow of opening a studio and publishing a product listing.

We tested the product with 5 real users and focused on clarity, ease of navigation, and whether the product reduces the confusion of selling through direct messages.

---

## Participants

| Participant | User Type        | Relevant Background                                           |
| ----------- | ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| User 1      | Handmade creator | Has sold handmade items informally through Instagram messages |
| User 2      | Handmade creator | Makes crafts but has not started selling yet                  |
| User 3      | Potential buyer  | Buys handmade or custom items online                          |
| User 4      | Student creator  | Interested in opening a small creative shop                   |
| User 5      | Potential buyer  | Uses social media to discover small creators                  |

---

## Test Method

Each participant was asked to complete the following tasks:

1. Open the Musa product.
2. Understand what the platform is for.
3. Browse products on the Explore page.
4. Open a product detail page.
5. Create or view a studio.
6. Try to understand how a creator would publish a product.
7. Explain what felt clear, confusing, or missing.

Testing was done through direct observation and follow-up questions. We recorded user comments, task completion issues, and repeated patterns.

---

## Key Findings

### Finding 1: Users understood the product faster when the wording focused on handmade creators

Several users understood the product better when Musa was described as a simple shop page for handmade creators, rather than as a general marketplace.

**Evidence:**
One participant said that “marketplace” sounded broad, but “a place where I can post my handmade products” was easier to understand.

**Impact:**
We made the product messaging more specific to handmade creators instead of using broad e-commerce language.

---

### Finding 2: Product cards needed clearer click behavior

Users expected product cards on the Explore page to open a product details page. In early testing, some users were unsure whether the card itself was clickable or whether they had to click a specific button.

**Evidence:**
Two users clicked on product images first, expecting to see more information.

**Impact:**
We changed the product cards so that clicking a product leads to a product detail page. This made the browsing flow feel more natural.

---

### Finding 3: Studio names needed to appear correctly on products

Users wanted to know which creator or studio made each product. When product cards showed a generic studio name, users said the product felt less trustworthy and less personal.

**Evidence:**
One participant said that seeing the real creator name would make the product feel more “handmade” and authentic.

**Impact:**
We updated the product display logic so product cards show the actual studio name instead of a generic placeholder name.

---

### Finding 4: Creators wanted the listing flow to feel simple and low-pressure

Creator participants said they would hesitate if the listing form felt too long or professional. They preferred a simple flow where they could add a product quickly and improve details later.

**Evidence:**
One creator participant said: “If I need everything perfect before posting, I probably won’t start.”

**Impact:**
We kept the product listing flow focused on essential information: product name, description, price, category, and image.

---

### Finding 5: Buyers expected visible prices and clear currency

Users wanted prices to be immediately understandable. If the currency was not clear, buyers were unsure whether the product was meant for the Georgian market or an international audience.

**Evidence:**
A buyer participant asked whether the price was in GEL or another currency.

**Impact:**
We decided to show prices in GEL to match the local market and make product cards clearer for buyers.

---

## Documented Design Change Made in Response

### Design Change: Product cards became clickable and more creator-specific

Based on usability testing, we changed the product browsing experience. Users expected to click on a product card to see more details, and they wanted to know which studio created the product.

Before testing, product cards were less clear because the click behavior was not obvious and some products appeared with a generic studio name. After testing, we updated the product card behavior so products open a detail page, and we improved studio display so products show the correct creator/studio name.

This change directly responded to repeated user behavior during testing, especially users clicking product images and asking who made the item.

---

## Summary of Results

| Area Tested           | Result                                       | Action Taken                 |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Product understanding | Mostly clear after handmade creator wording  | Improved messaging           |
| Explore page          | Users expected product cards to open details | Product cards made clickable |
| Product trust         | Users wanted real studio names               | Studio name display improved |
| Creator listing flow  | Users preferred a simple low-pressure form   | Listing flow kept simple     |
| Pricing clarity       | Users wanted currency shown clearly          | Prices shown in GEL          |

---

## Conclusion

The usability test showed that Musa’s core concept is understandable and relevant for handmade creators, especially creators who currently depend on social media messages. The most important improvement from testing was making the product browsing flow clearer by allowing product cards to open detail pages and showing the correct studio identity.

The findings confirmed that Musa should stay focused on simplicity, creator trust, and reducing the chaos of selling handmade products through direct messages.
