## 1. Purpose of this Notice

This Privacy Notice explains how Team Nemesis (“Musa”, “we”, “us”, or “our”) collects, uses, stores, shares, and protects personal data when people visit or use the Musa handmade-product marketplace.

Musa is currently an early-stage academic MVP that allows creators to register, create a studio, publish handmade-product listings, and make those listings visible in a public Explore feed.

This notice applies to:

- visitors who browse Musa;
- registered creators;
- people who contact the Musa team for help, feedback, or privacy requests.

The current MVP does **not** process online payments, delivery details, card information, or completed marketplace orders. This notice must be updated before any such functionality is introduced.

## 5. Public Information

Creators choose which studio and product details to publish. Once published, this information can be viewed by anyone who accesses Musa.

Public information may include:

- creator or display name;
- studio name and description;
- studio or profile image;
- product title, description, price, category, and images;
- social-media or contact details voluntarily entered into a public field.

Creators should publish only information and images that they have the right to share. Removing information from Musa does not guarantee immediate removal of copies previously saved, shared, indexed, or cached by other people or services. Musa will remove or restrict its own active copy and request deletion from its processors where appropriate.

Musa may use browser storage or cookies that are strictly necessary for:

- keeping a user signed in;

- remembering essential service state;

Any non-essential analytics technology that stores or accesses information on a user’s device should be activated only after valid consent where consent is legally required. Users may withdraw analytics consent at any time through the available preference mechanism.

Musa does not currently use advertising cookies or cross-site behavioural tracking.

## 9. Data Retention

We retain personal data only for as long as necessary for the purpose for which it was collected, unless a longer period is required to comply with law, resolve disputes, maintain security, or establish legal claims.

| Data category | Retention period |
|---|---|
| Active account, studio, and product-listing data | For as long as the account remains active |
| Deleted account, studio, and listing data in the active database | Deleted or anonymised within 30 days after a valid deletion request or account closure, unless retention is legally required |
| Backup copies | Removed through normal backup rotation within up to 30 additional days |
| Authentication sessions and tokens | Until expiry, logout, revocation, or account deletion |
| Technical, access, security, and error logs | Up to 90 days, unless needed longer to investigate a security incident or legal claim |
| Basic product-usage and analytics records | Up to 12 months; aggregated or anonymised information may be kept longer because it no longer identifies a user |
| Support and feedback communications | Up to 12 months after the matter is closed |
| Privacy-rights requests and response records | Up to 3 years after closure to demonstrate compliance |
| Consent and withdrawal records | For the period consent is relied upon and for up to 1 year after withdrawal, or longer where legally necessary to demonstrate compliance |
| Security-incident and breach records | Up to 5 years after the incident is closed |
| Public content temporarily cached by the hosting/CDN provider | Normally removed or refreshed through cache expiration, and targeted for deletion within 30 days after removal from Musa |

When a retention period ends, data will be securely deleted, anonymised, or isolated from ordinary use.

## 10. Data Security

Musa applies proportionate technical and organisational measures to protect personal data, including:

- authenticated access to non-public account functions;
- password hashing and secure authentication managed through Supabase;
- database access controls and row-level security;
- HTTPS encryption for data in transit;
- access restrictions based on team responsibilities;
- limited administrator access;
- secure handling of API keys and environment variables;
- software updates and dependency review;
- logging and review of security-relevant events;
- backup and incident-response procedures.

No online service can guarantee absolute security. Users should use a strong, unique password and should not share authentication credentials.

## 11. User Rights

Subject to applicable law and any lawful exceptions, users have the following rights.

### 11.1 Right of Access

Users may ask whether Musa processes their personal data and request a copy of that data, together with information about how it is used.

### 11.2 Right to Rectification

Users may ask Musa to correct inaccurate personal data or complete incomplete data. Creators may also be able to edit some information directly in their account or studio.

### 11.3 Right to Erasure

Users may request deletion of personal data when it is no longer needed, consent has been withdrawn and no other lawful basis exists, or the data has been processed unlawfully.

Deletion may be refused or limited where retention is necessary to comply with law, protect freedom of expression, establish or defend legal claims, prevent abuse, or meet another lawful exception.

### 11.4 Right to Restriction of Processing

Users may ask Musa to temporarily limit processing while the accuracy, lawfulness, or basis for processing is reviewed.

### 11.5 Right to Data Portability

Where processing is automated and based on consent or contract, users may request personal data they provided in a structured, commonly used, machine-readable format. Where technically feasible, they may request transfer to another controller.

### 11.6 Right to Object

Users may object to processing based on legitimate interests. Musa will stop the processing unless it demonstrates compelling legitimate grounds that override the user’s interests, rights, and freedoms, or the processing is needed for legal claims.

Users may object to direct marketing at any time. Musa will stop direct-marketing processing after receiving the objection.

### 11.7 Right to Withdraw Consent

Where processing is based on consent, users may withdraw consent at any time. Withdrawal does not affect the lawfulness of processing performed before withdrawal.

### 11.8 Rights Relating to Automated Decisions

Musa does not currently make decisions producing legal or similarly significant effects based solely on automated processing. If this changes, this notice will be updated and users will receive any rights required by law.

### 11.9 Right to Complain

Users may complain directly to Team Nemesis through the privacy contact email.

Users may also submit a complaint to the competent Georgian data-protection supervisory authority or, where the GDPR applies, to the supervisory authority in the EU/EEA country where they live, work, or believe an infringement occurred.

### 13.2 Internal Procedure

When a suspected breach is reported or detected, Team Nemesis will:

1. **Report and escalate immediately.** The team member discovering the incident must notify the Privacy and Security Lead without delay.
2. **Contain the incident.** Revoke exposed credentials, restrict affected access, isolate vulnerable services, and preserve relevant evidence.
3. **Record the incident.** Document when it occurred, when it was discovered, affected systems, data categories, likely number of people affected, actions taken, and decisions made.
4. **Assess risk.** Evaluate the sensitivity and amount of data, ease of identification, possible harm, affected users, and whether the data was encrypted or otherwise protected.
5. **Contact processors.** Notify and coordinate with Supabase, Cloudflare, or any other affected processor as necessary.
6. **Notify the supervisory authority.** Where notification is legally required, notify the competent authority as soon as possible and no later than **72 hours after becoming aware of the breach**. If notification is delayed, document and explain the reason.
7. **Notify affected people.** Where the breach is likely to create a high or significant risk to people’s rights and freedoms, notify affected users without undue delay in clear language. The notice will describe the incident, likely consequences, mitigation steps, and contact method.
8. **Recover and remediate.** Correct the vulnerability, restore secure operation, monitor for further misuse, and reset credentials where appropriate.
9. **Review.** Conduct a post-incident review and update technical controls, procedures, and training.

All personal data incidents will be documented, including incidents that do not require external notification and the reasons for that decision.
